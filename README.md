# Supply Chain Intelligence

Independent AI-assisted portfolio prototype for inventory planning, demand forecasting and supplier performance review. It uses 24 fictional SKUs, 16 weeks of generated demand per SKU, and four fictional suppliers. No real company data is included.

## Run
Open `index.html` with `model.js` in the same folder. No API keys or dependencies. For GitHub Pages, publish `main` / root.

## Planning model
- Forecast: choose a 4- or 8-week moving average using the lower mean absolute error (MAE) on the last four rolling one-step validation forecasts. Fit the selected moving average to the latest weeks and apply the scenario demand multiplier. MAE remains in original historical units; this small validation sample is not an independent test set.
- ABC: sort by modeled annual consumption value (weekly forecast × 52 × unit cost); classify A until cumulative value reaches 80%, B until 95%, C thereafter. The item crossing a boundary belongs to the preceding class.
- XYZ: historical coefficient of variation (population standard deviation / mean); X ≤ 0.2, Y ≤ 0.5, Z above 0.5.
- Safety stock: ceil(z × weekly demand standard deviation × square root of lead time in weeks). The selected cycle service level determines z. Demand is assumed independent and lead time deterministic.
- Reorder point: ceil(forecast × lead time + safety stock). Inventory position = on-hand + inbound (no backorders in this model).
- Purchase recommendation: if inventory position falls below the reorder point, order enough to reach the order-up-to level (forecast × [lead time + buffer] plus safety stock for the combined period). Otherwise recommend zero. This is a simplified reorder-point policy, not a periodic review simulation.
- Stockout risk: on-hand below forecast lead-time demand. This is a screening signal, not a probability; inbound dates are unknown. Excess: inventory position above 150% of order-up-to level, provided no higher-priority risk applies.
- Supplier on-time and fill rates: fictional fixed historical metrics, not generated by the scenario.

## Scenario experiment
Increase demand, increase supplier lead times, and compare recommended purchasing spend. Raise the target service level to see the trade-off between protection and working capital. Review AZ items and suppliers with poor delivery performance before placing orders.

## Tests
Run `node test.js` (Node.js 18+). Covers 240 scenarios and three additional checks: determinism, invalid service input, and monotonic safety stock across service targets.

## Limitations
No live ERP integration, measured supplier outcomes, purchase constraints, stock aging, backorders, multi-echelon inventory, or stochastic service-level validation. Forecasting is statistical and rule-based, not generative AI. Synthetic results demonstrate methods rather than measured business improvements. Future work: actual demand history, out-of-sample validation, inbound schedules, MOQ and budget constraints, lead-time variability, and supplier risk modeling.
